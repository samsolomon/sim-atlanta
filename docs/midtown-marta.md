## North Line portal and open guideway

North of Arts Center the Red and Gold lines stay in tunnel until a cut just east of the Brookwood interchange. The archived OSM extract maps two open North Line mains from the board's north edge (z −5700) south to tunnel mouths at (343.2, −5137.8) and (348.2, −5140.5). City now runs scheduled or live Red and Gold consists on those two mains. Train identity, position and tier come from the existing train state. The track assignment (right-hand running) is inferred. Cars are clipped at the portal face, so a train is never drawn in the covered tunnel or beyond the board. Red trips that run only north of Lindbergh never reach the board, so none is placed.

Aerial imagery shows a two-track cut with retaining walls ending at a cut-and-cover box under the trees. The 3DEP grid gives 264.2 m NAVD88 on the trackbed at (345, −5140), 269.6 m on the east lip of the cut and 274.0 m over the tunnel 20 m south. That 10 m of cover is consistent with the mapped tunnel. City's rendered ground matches the grid on both tiers (264.2, 270.9 and 274.0 m at 0, 10 and 20 m behind the mouth), so the rear of the 8 m hood meets the rising slope near its roof. No ground-level day or night photograph of this portal could be found. General web searches, a Wikimedia Commons search and Jim Bell's MARTA Northeast photo pages turned up only aerial views. Commons holds station categories but no portal photograph. [nycsubway.org on the tunnel north of Arts Center](https://nycsubway.org/wiki/MARTA_Provisions) The hood is therefore an illustrative concrete headwall with a recessed louver panel and a centre wall between the tracks, matching the single box seen from above. Its face stands at the more advanced of the two mapped mouths, where both tracks clip. The 10.4 m width, 5.1 m height and 8 m depth are estimates. Near the guideway City adds the note "Inferred MARTA track" after the Crescent note. It has no lighting, because none was documented. Its base follows the rendered trackbed mesh rather than a surveyed sill. ## Electric Saturation fan house

The windowless concrete MARTA building at 1008 Peachtree Walk stands over the North Line between the Midtown and Arts Center stations. In 2024 Jessie Unterhalter and Katey Truhn painted it as *Electric Saturation*. From the south-east the photographs show hard-edged rainbow arcs, discs, triangles, crescents and vertical bands on the south and east faces. A low shaft painted in navy and colour stripes wraps the corner. From the north along the Walk they show the full east face, a navy north end with blue arcs, two roll-up doors (one orange) and a black welded-mesh fence around a paved apron. High horizontal louvres pierce the south face. [Midtown Alliance, south-east corner](https://img.ctykit.com/cdn/ga-midtown/images/tr:w-1800/10-electric-saturation.png) · [MARTA Artbound, east face from the north](https://itsmarta.com/images/artbound/Pictures/Jessie%20&%20Katey.jpg) · [Project page](https://www.midtownatl.com/go/electric-saturation) · [Urbanize Atlanta, south face across 10th Street](https://atlanta.urbanize.city/post/midtown-art-walk-has-officially-arrived-photos)

The parapet is level, an estimated 5.6 m above the highest corner and 7.6 m above the lowest. The footings reach 0.4 m below the lowest corner. The shaft's 1.7 m height, the door sizes, the 1.2 m fence and the louvre position are estimates. City therefore lights only the small wall packs seen at about 3.5 m on the east and south faces. The mural is unlit.

## North Line bed between the portal and the north edge

North of the portal the 3DEP grid shows the North Line riding a crest narrower than its 4.2 m tread. The generic freight strip took its height only from its two edges, so the rendered terrain rose through the middle of the tread. Before the fix, sampling every metre at nine offsets across both mains found 1,605 breakthroughs in brookwood-station on both tiers. The worst was 0.71 m at (723, −5698), and 291 were deeper than 0.2 m. Each MARTA ground section now levels onto the highest rendered ground across its full width. Ballast faces close its raised sides. The lift fades out over the last 6 m before a bridge abutment or the tunnel mouth, so those joints and the portal sill keep their previous heights. Joint steps stay under 0.35 m, against up to 0.20 m before, measured 0.3 m either side of each node. Away from the fades no sample breaks through. Inside them the worst is 0.19 m, down from 0.71 m. The bed sits 0.67 m above the centreline ground at the 95th percentile. The levelled section is an inference from bare earth, not a surveyed bed. ## North Line guideway and spans

From the portal to the board's north edge, City now draws the North Line from its own module, with one designed profile shared by both tracks. The generic rail strips skip the North Line runs, which stay in the row data for crossings and placement. On bare earth the ballast tread rides 0.13 m above the highest rendered ground between the outer rails. Where both shoulders stand higher, as in the walled cut at the portal, it takes only the ground under the rails. Wherever a mapped road crosses under a span, rail level stays at least 7.1 m above that road's ground. That leaves 4.9 m beneath the 2.2 m deck and girder. A span over Clear Creek, which has no road beneath, simply joins its banks. A grade-limited envelope (3.5% at most) with a 20 m vertical curve joins the pieces, so each span meets its banks without a step. The first 6 m inside the portal hood take their height from the cut beyond, because there the grid blends in the hillside over the tunnel. Trains and the portal read their height from the same profile.

The generic bridges had taken their end heights from 3DEP nodes that fall on the abutment walls. Through the rendered grid, Bridge A's tread fell to 255.5 m at its north end and then climbed 5 m to the bank in 20 m, about a 25% grade. Over I-85 southbound it left about 3 m beneath its own depth. Bridge B sat level 2.5 m below banks at about 259 m. The designed profile leaves 4.9 m under the soffit at all twelve road crossings under Bridge A. Both tracks agree on the shared deck to within 0.02 m. On bare earth the bed sits a median 0.3 m above the centreline ground. The 95th percentile is 1.9 m, set by the approach embankments, whose retaining walls rise up to 5 m beside the GA 13 ramps.

**Bridge A** (OSM ways 54044472 and 319512643, 230–235 m, `electrified=rail`, 750 V third rail) crosses the GA 13 ramps and carriageways and both directions of I-85. Two daytime photographs from I-85 southbound show one weathered cast-in-place concrete box girder, about 2 m deep, with sloped outer webs. The deck slab overhangs thinly, and steel pipe railings run along both edges. The piers are plain rectangular single columns, one with a flared cap, and a tall panelled retaining wall forms the north abutment. No catenary appears. Both photographs face south-west from below, so the deck top, walkways and third-rail side were never seen, and those details are inferred. **Bridge B** (ways 429222717 and 429222719, 88–90 m) crosses Clear Creek beside the Norfolk Southern tracks. No photograph of it was found, so its form repeats Bridge A's and is illustrative. Neither MARTA structure appears in the National Bridge Inventory. The neighbouring I-85 and SR 13 bridges over Clear Creek date from 1983–84, the same campaign as the North Line's 1984 opening to Lindbergh. No night photograph of either span was found, so they carry no lighting. ### Piers, railings and abutments

Piers stand only where neither a rendered Connector lane (with a 1 m margin) nor a mapped road (at its drawn width plus 1 m) lies under the 2.4 × 2.2 m footprint. They are spaced 20–45 m apart. Where the carriageways leave no gore within 45 m, the span runs on to the nearest clear one. Under Bridge A the piers fall at 114, 156, 206 and 270 m along the track, in the gores between the GA 13 and I-85 carriageways. The photographs place gore piers about 65–75, 105–115 and 150–165 m from the south abutment, which would be about 119–129, 159–169 and 204–219 m along the track. Bridge B has one pier, at 524 m. Pier positions, sizes and railing heights are estimates, and Bridge B repeats Bridge A's details without photographs.
