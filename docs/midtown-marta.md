# Midtown MARTA stations

## North Avenue

The low entrance at West Peachtree and North Avenue has a concrete fascia spanning an open approach. The street pylon and recessed ceiling lights are visible from the south. The view back from the other end shows two rows of pyramidal skylights and a colored fascia facing the plaza. The plaza has square planters and a stage built from a shipping container. At the next block, three mapped doorways lead into the existing Tower Square building. ## Midtown station

The Peachtree Place entrance has a deep concrete waffle canopy. The ends of its transverse beams remain visible from the street. Glazed doors and a broad opening alternate with concrete piers. The renovated plaza photograph shows painted plant forms on the piers, benches and low green seating mounds. Across 10th Street two smaller head houses cover the separate entrances. The pavilion outline (OSM 230593407) is archived as a station and missing from the generic row buildings. The model uses that 66 × 43 m outline together with mapped ways 235006952 and 235006968 and the two pedestrian plazas. Local 3DEP grid nodes give 292.1 m NAVD88 at (480, −3025) and 292.8 m at both (465, −3085) and (500, −3085). The canopy soffit sits an estimated 4.35 m above the highest footprint vertex. Rib spacing, art colors, stair enclosure height and lighting are inferred from appearance. ## Arts Center

At the Arts Center Way entrance the concrete roof projects over an upper-level plaza. The street view shows the pylon beyond the trees. The other angle resolves the low parapet, glazing and guardrail. The exterior night photograph shows the fascia dark above a brightly lit geometric mural and yellow-green illuminated seating risers. The model abstracts those colors and places the riser lights on the plaza side. The western canopy serves the bus loop. [Street corner](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arts_Center_Transit_Station_(MARTA).JPG) · [Entrance side](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marta_arts_center.jpg) · [Photographer's night visit](https://atlanta.urbanize.city/post/photos-how-lighting-additions-are-enlivening-midtown-night) · [MARTA station profile](https://itsmarta.com/uploadedFiles/More/Transit_Oriented_Development/Station%20Profile%20Rev%202017-11-03%20Arts%20Center.pdf)

OSM ways 269637886 and 269637571 provide the roughly 110 m passenger building and 124 m bus structure, measured along their north–south extents. The plaza boundary comes from way 1035248909. At the east entrance the nearby 3DEP node (450, −3930) is 288.4 m NAVD88. The nearest nodes to the main building's corners range from 281.9 to 289.1 m. The inventory found the generic roof 3.3 m underground at (451, −3933). The east entrance now sets the passenger roof datum, and the piers below it each reach the local rendered ground. A ray cast through the audit point verifies more than 3 m of clearance above the high- and low-detail surfaces. A 3.6 m soffit above the upper entrance, a 0.96 m fascia, the deck thickness, canopy bays, seating and night intensity are visual estimates. ## North Line portal and open guideway

North of Arts Center the Red and Gold lines stay in tunnel until a cut just east of the Brookwood interchange. The archived OSM extract maps two open North Line mains from the board's north edge (z −5700) south to tunnel mouths at (343.2, −5137.8) and (348.2, −5140.5). City now runs scheduled or live Red and Gold consists on those two mains. Train identity, position and tier come from the existing train state. The track assignment (right-hand running) is inferred. Cars are clipped at the portal face, so a train is never drawn in the covered tunnel or beyond the board. Red trips that run only north of Lindbergh never reach the board, so none is placed.

Aerial imagery shows a two-track cut with retaining walls ending at a cut-and-cover box under the trees. The 3DEP grid gives 264.2 m NAVD88 on the trackbed at (345, −5140), 269.6 m on the east lip of the cut and 274.0 m over the tunnel 20 m south. That 10 m of cover is consistent with the mapped tunnel. No ground-level day or night photograph of this portal could be found. Searches of Commons, railfan sites and MARTA material turned up only aerial views. The hood is therefore an illustrative concrete headwall with a recessed louver panel. Its 5.8 m width, 5.1 m height and 8 m depth per track are estimates. It has no lighting, because none was documented. Its base follows the rendered trackbed mesh rather than a surveyed sill.
