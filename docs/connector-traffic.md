# Illustrative Connector traffic in City

City draws the Downtown Connector from directed carriageways, ramp movements and dated stripe measurements. The downtown board contains 1.26 km of northbound and 1.41 km of southbound mainline in two separate crossings. With King Memorial and Sweet Auburn loaded, those lengths reach 2.19 and 2.29 km. These lengths include covered intervals and exclude ramps. The gap between Sweet Auburn and the northern crossing closes when the northeast corner of the square board is available. ## Lane geometry

The original two Georgia State sections constrain all seven positions per direction through 153 m. The north and south registers add 22 and 66 transects respectively. Each measured edge belongs to a directed way. Between cuts, the renderer follows its bends and interpolates the offset to the stripes. Unsurveyed branch intervals use the mapped lane count and an estimated 3.55 m pitch. Nominal mainline shoulders are 2.2 m inside and 2.8 m outside. Imagery shows local departures from both values. The narrow concrete divider is modelled at a schematic 0.84 m high. The broad median at Freedom leaves room for separate ramps. [Original section](connector-road-footprint.md) · [Shared carriageways](../web/src/terrain/connector-corridor.ts)

At Georgia State five northbound mainline lanes and a two-lane feeder come together. Another entrance adds an eighth position near Decatur. Southbound traffic has a choice lane at the I‑20 exit. The managed exit at Piedmont leaves the inside northbound position. The southbound managed entrance near Edgewood joins five general lanes before a separate right entrance makes seven. Between Freedom and Pine there are seven stripes per direction, falling to six on the continuing mainline after the decision points. Ramp and mainline stripe endpoints share a coordinate at a choice or merge. The vehicle centre falls halfway between the same painted edges.

## What the cars represent

GDOT station 121-5472 gives a calculated 2025 annual average of **237,000 vehicles/day, both directions combined**, near Georgia State. The City simulation uses this value as a corridor-wide demand proxy and applies the weekday/weekend hourly shape from FHWA TMAS 2023 Atlanta station 000009 at Freedom Parkway. Nearby annual estimates range up to 369,000 at Decatur. Traffic on screen is illustrative. Lane choices, direction shares, vehicle mix, 18 m/s speed and individual positions are modelled. [GDOT stations and methodology](connector-south-survey.md#gdot-station-cross-check) · [Hourly profile](../pipeline/scripts/vignette_traffic.py)

Each disconnected visible approach starts a new illustrative flow using half the reference volume for its direction. Each entering lane receives a share of 1 divided by the larger of six or its approach lane count. A separate ramp origin adds its own share. A choice lane divides that share between its outgoing paths. Paths passing Georgia State are normalized together to one directional reference flow. The board uses one budget of 48 cars on low or 120 on high. Attaching an adjacent board rebuilds that single set of routes across the new footprint. A second disconnected crossing can therefore receive the same proxy. The budget calculation sums expected cars present over each route’s travel time. It produces no estimate of unique vehicles passing through downtown. An absent hourly profile leaves traffic unavailable.

The simulator clips each lane to the union of loaded rectangles, follows the road through covered intervals with the car hidden, and dissolves cars over the final 18 m at exposed board and ramp endpoints. All three wheelbase samples use the carrying road. Cars yield to bus footprints and other cars at the same level. Sweet Auburn uses lidar-height deck profiles and preserves the Edgewood left-turn pocket at street level.
