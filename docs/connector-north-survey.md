# Downtown Connector from Freedom Parkway toward Midtown

Between the Freedom entrance and the Pine Street exit, the 2024 Fulton orthophoto shows about **24–25 m of striped travel area in each direction**. Seven marked positions fit that width. Southeast of the Freedom split and merge, the six-position mainlines use about 21 m each. Farther northwest, the two directions change width at different places. Each direction has one inside managed lane. The shoulder, the median and detached ramps are outside these counts. **Method.** On November 22, 2026, I inspected Fulton County Pictometry 2024 layer 3 in Orca and sampled its georeferenced image exports at 0.25 m per output pixel. I sectioned the image every 35 m along the SB OSM reference, perpendicular to the smoothed travel direction, and retained exposed cuts around junctions and changes in median width. The dashed and solid stripes identify the through path and the approximate edges. Vehicle shadows, shadows from decks, and interrupted paint make individual edge picks uncertain by roughly 1–2 m. Across a taper, the outer edge of the continuing lanes is inferred from adjacent marks. The shoulder width and the exact point where a driver can change lanes are less certain. N42 crosses a partial bridge shadow. Widths there carry the neighbouring lane pitch. The decimal coordinates keep both sides aligned to the same transect. ## Ramp movements, shoulders and stacking

- **Northbound from Freedom.** The mainline arrives at the downtown east edge with six striped lanes including the managed lane. A separately mapped two-lane approach runs beside it. The approach narrows to one entering lane toward the merge at City (799.81, −1042.64). Seven marked positions continue northwest. At the Pine Street exit near (569.74, −1296.05), the rightmost exits and the adjacent lane has a choice. Six continue on the mainline and two can take the ramp. On the southeast approach toward Sweet Auburn a managed lane branches into the median, and the continuing SB way drops to five. - **Shoulders and median.** At N20 and N26 the inside yellow lines are roughly 5–6 m apart. There is a narrow concrete divider and approximately 2–3 m of paved inside shoulder on either side. The outside white line by the wall has roughly a car-width to a full lane-width of shoulder on exposed approaches, about 2–4 m. At the entering and exiting ramps this margin opens into a wedge of pavement. Follow the branch edge there. Beyond N35 the grassy separation expands to over 30 m, with flyovers or managed-lane pavement within it. The exposed roadway has its own narrow shoulder on each side. ## Inventory cross-check and traffic

Georgia's 2024 HPMS submission records **12 through lanes including two managed** on the I-75 inventory alignment near these northern cuts and 355,000 vehicles/day near Peachtree. It gives no directional through split. This agrees with six continuing positions per side in the narrow portions. The aerial and directed ramp graph supply the extra outer positions through the junctions. [FHWA HPMS section](https://services.arcgis.com/xOi1kZaI0eWDREZv/ArcGIS/rest/services/HPMS_National_Current/FeatureServer/0/3439997) · [FHWA lane definitions](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/hpms/fieldmanual/page05.cfm)

GDOT's 2025 workbooks give 357,000 vehicles/day at mainline station **121-5477** (33.765064, −84.384814) and 321,000 at **121-5476** (33.761460, −84.380690). Both are calculated annual averages for the combined mainline. Nearby ramp records are much smaller. **121-R027** reports 20,000 and **121-R631** reports 11,600, each expanded from a short-term count. These sources do not resolve the time, lane or speed of any individual vehicle in the scene.
