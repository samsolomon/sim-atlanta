# Five Points Plaza, 40 Marietta Street

From the square, each floor reads as a ribbon of pale concrete around a strip of recessed glass. At Forsyth and Marietta, the ribbons curve around the corners and broaden where they meet the columns. Chuck Robisch of Tomberlin and Sheetz designed the 17-story First Federal Savings and Loan tower, completed in 1964, with six pentagonal perimeter columns and post-tensioned beams. The former bank stands apart from the glass office towers on either side. [Georgia architectural survey, p. 37](https://dca.georgia.gov/document/document/modern-downtown-atlanta/download) · [Owner’s exterior photograph](https://jordacheenterprises.com/project/5-points-atlanta/) · [Forsyth sidewalk view](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&viewpoint=33.755177,-84.391524&heading=110&pitch=35&fov=88)

A 1967 aerial gives a second reading. The pale mass on the back is a continuous blank wall with rounded ends. Its head rises beside a rectangular raised roof, leaving a lower strip visible around the edge. The November 2026 City study pairs this roof view with the contemporary street elevations. [Street view of the blank spine](https://www.flickr.com/photos/sayednairb/3877349818/) · [Atlanta History Center, 1967](https://dlg.usg.edu/record/geh_atlcc_94) · [Marietta Street view](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&viewpoint=33.755321,-84.390756&heading=234&pitch=36&fov=88)
