# Sam Nunn Atlanta Federal Center

Kohn Pedersen Fox’s west elevation shows three broad fields of blue-gray glass framed in pale stone. The offset end blades make the tower look quite different from the narrow end. Across the courtyard, the glass mid-rise, the bridge over Forsyth and the punched masonry windows of the former Rich’s department store each have their own scale. The architect counts 24 stories in the main tower, ten in the mid-rise and eight in the bridge building. [Architect’s street and plaza photographs](https://www.kpf.com/project/atlanta-federal-center) · [View across the Gulch](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sam_Nunn_Atlanta_Federal_Building.jpg) · [Alabama Street frontage](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&viewpoint=33.753585,-84.392075&heading=233&pitch=8&fov=88) · [Annex corner](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sam_Nunn_Atlanta_Federal_Center_Annex,_Atlanta,_GA_(46751138014).jpg)

The 2025 Garnett plaza presentation includes a dusk aerial looking north across South Downtown. The three bands on the tower remain legible long after the street lights come on. In the 2018 stadium photograph, the dark three-banded Nunn slab is immediately left of the much lighter Russell tower. Scattered windows show through. Individual lit rooms in City are illustrative. [Night skyline from the stadium district](https://data2.nssmag.com/images/galleries/18547/DtQXGDsXcAA-K0V-jpg-large.jpeg) [Atlanta Downtown, page 26](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c6c05c2e666694eb6efcd07/t/68c201e2c3e96b70eef3e771/1757544930509/Garnett_20Station-sm.pdf.pdf#page=26)
