# One Coca-Cola Plaza

The Coca-Cola headquarters tower (completed 1979; SKYDB gives 1980) on North Avenue uses the mapped outline `osm:way:536717630` (about 47 × 48 m, mapped 122.8 m and 29 floors). The April 2019 lidar puts the roof at p50 116.0, p95 122.8 and maximum 125.2 m above grade. The model reads those as the deck inside the parapet, the central penthouse and the four corner piers.

## References

- [SKYDB building page and three exterior photographs](https://www.skydb.net/building/204849954/one-coca-cola-plaza/): a single face with the blank top band, a distant view over a highway fence, and a two-face close view showing the corner shaft rising above the parapet.
- [Wikimedia Commons, northeast view from the Guggenheim building, 17 July 2016](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Coca-Cola_Tower_and_Guggenheim_Building,_Atlanta,_Northeast_view_20160717_1.jpg): the second angle. Two faces show the same eggcrate grid, the solid corner shafts and the blank sign band.
- [Wikimedia Commons category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Coca-Cola_headquarters) for further daytime views.

The facade is cream precast in a deep eggcrate grid. Each face has 13 window bays between solid corner shafts, under a blank band about two floors high that carries the red script signs. The signs are logos and are left off.

## Estimates

- Bay pitch: (47.2 m − 2 × 2.9 m corner shafts) / 13 ≈ 3.2 m. Piers are 1.44 m wide, so the opening is about 55% of the bay.
- Floors: 27 window courses at 3.86 m above a 7.2 m lobby and a 0.9 m transfer band. The grid tops out near 112 m. The sign band and parapet rise to 119.2 m, the penthouse to 122.8 m and the corner shafts to 124.6 m.
- Window recess is stylised at 0.75 m.
- The ground floor is hidden by the campus buildings in every photograph, so it is estimated: glazing set back 1.6 m, with four columns per face carrying the grid down to grade.

## Lighting

No full-night exterior of this tower was available. The 2011 anniversary projection (AJC, Adweek) was a one-off event and is not ordinary lighting. The night view uses office occupancy: a sparse, stable set of warm panes (about 3% of windows, from skin-kit `litHash`; the first version's linear rule stacked them on diagonals, see [before and after](night-before-after.jpg)), a warm lobby behind the recessed glass and a dark crown. The lit red signs are omitted with the logos.
