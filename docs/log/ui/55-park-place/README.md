# 55 Park Place

On the Park Place approach, two three-bay stone wings frame a pair of stacked glass atriums. The upper floors recede in short steps above the glass. The reverse view from Auburn shows the pale wall of the taller eastern service core. The deployed City view had a single roof and horizontal window bands around the whole footprint. Georgia’s preservation survey identifies this as a 19-story Skidmore, Owings & Merrill office building. Georgia State identifies the current campus address with its Robinson College of Business. [Production before](production-before.jpg) · [State preservation survey, Appendix C-35](https://dca.georgia.gov/document/resources/modern-downtown-atlanta-multiple-properties-form/download#page=174) · [University building record](https://calendar.gsu.edu/rcb_55_park_place)

**Dimensions.** The downtown building record gives 86.868 m height and 19 floors. Its street frame measures roughly 55 m from north to south. The southern and northern office wings extend about 55 m east from Park Place, and the service core projects a further 5 m. The central notch is about 17.6 m wide. Within the footprint, 8,924 returns from the 2018 lidar locate the broad office roof around 399.6 m NAVD88 and the main service roof at 405.9 m. The four lower Park Place ledges rise through approximately 383.5, 387.5, 391.4 and 395.4 m. The aerial shows a dark rectangular skylight behind them. Returns below the skylight cluster near 359.5 m. The visible glass plane and its mullion spacing are estimated from the aerial. [USGS 2018 lidar](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/usgs-lidar-public/GA_Statewide_B2_2018/ept.json) · [Esri overhead photograph](https://server.arcgisonline.com/ArcGIS/rest/services/World_Imagery/MapServer/export?bbox=-9394002.487219501,3996018.812520571,-9393879.67002223,3996141.629717841&bboxSR=3857&imageSR=3857&size=800,800&format=jpg&f=image)

**Facade and lighting.** The Park Place photograph shows a glazed lobby, narrow double lines in the stone between floors and deep window recesses on either side. The Auburn approach shows a continuous office elevation beside the service wall. The contractor’s evening photograph gives a head-on view of the two atriums. Several contiguous interior bays catch warm light, particularly at the lobby and in the lower atrium. City uses that contrast for an illustrative night treatment. The office lights, atrium depths below the measured ledges, glass colour and step dimensions are estimates.
