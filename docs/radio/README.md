# Radio: how the music was made

- **Coded track:** `Something Small (Demo)` is synthesized in Python (numpy/scipy) from the
  November 2026 SimCity-style sketch: piano ostinato, warm pad, flute-like lead, soft beat,
  F major, 100 BPM, with intro, A, B, a drumless bell break, a lifted A and an outro.
- **ElevenLabs Music** (`music_v2_5`, instrumental). Prompts describe instruments, mood,
  tempo and structure, and never name artists, songs, labels or games. Three later tracks
  used chunked composition plans; v2.5 honoured the instruments but mostly ignored
  "drums drop out" sections.
- **Stations** were Sam's idea (2026-11-24): flip between them like car radio in a game;
  the first one follows the scene clock.
- **Covers:** one per release or fictional artist, generated by a Codex agent, reviewed at 64 px.
  See `covers/README.md` for prompts and the alternates that weren't picked.
- **Player spec** (compact one-row player, crossfade, stations): `docs/ui-controls-brief.md`,
  section Radio, on the UI branch.

## Long Haul 103.9

Axle & Pine plays the two shuffles on *Sunday Shoes*. Nell Tiller plays the acoustic single *Porch Light*. All three instrumentals were generated with ElevenLabs Music v2.5.

- **Sunday Shoes**. Instrumental truck-driving country shuffle, 120 BPM in E, warm and nimble. A clean electric guitar picks a distinctive two-bar low-string hook from beat one. Upright bass and a brushed snare carry the shuffle. Pedal steel answers the guitar at the ends of phrases. Play a complete ten-bar opening that can stand alone for a twenty-second highway film, then open up into a second chorus, a steel solo and a tidy turnaround. Small live band, lots of space, no vocals.
- **Porch Light**. Instrumental country-soul ballad, slow 6/8, warm Wurlitzer chords and fingerpicked acoustic guitar. A singing pedal steel melody hangs behind the beat, with soft bass and a restrained rim click. An intimate dusk drive home from a loading dock in Georgia. A new answering melody midway, ending on a held major-sixth chord. Earthy small-room sound, no vocals.
- **Somebody Owes Me a Dance**. Instrumental honky-tonk electric country shuffle in A major, a relaxed but propulsive 118 BPM in swung 4/4. Start straight away with the band on a short, cheeky two-bar clean bridge-pickup electric guitar lick: an open-string low note, two clipped high double stops and room for a reply. A round alternating root-fifth bass, light snare backbeat and soft rhythm acoustic keep a steady two-step. Pedal steel slides an answering phrase between guitar lines. Give the B section a descending bass line and a longer, sweeter steel melody, then trade short phrases, bring the opening lick back, end together on a clipped sixth chord. Keep the guitar out front, a little amp spring reverb, close dry drums, the warmth and dynamics of four players on a small wooden stage. Play in four throughout. Instrumental, no spoken or sung vocals.
